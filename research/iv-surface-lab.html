Implied Volatility Information in Variance Forecasting

Implied-variance information, forecast calibration, and robustness across horizons.

Research update · October 13, 2026

OptionsVolatility forecasting
Repository ↗October 2026 research update ↗Aggregate comparisons ↗Initial data audit ↗

Research question

Does 30-day SPX implied variance add information beyond log-HAR, and does lower forecast loss imply correct calibration?

Work completed

Compared HAR and HAR+IV with chronological label-maturity controls; evaluated log-MSE, QLIKE and calibration; tested recursive corrections, stale IV inputs and expiry-node sensitivity.

Data and design

15.6M SPX option records over 2010–2023 and SPY Garman–Klass variance proxies augmented with squared overnight returns. Original 2023 historical holdout; later calibration uses already viewed history.

Recorded findings

Adding IV reduced historical-holdout log-variance MSE by 24.3% / 35.6% at 5 / 21 sessions. Exploratory recursive calibration narrowed five-session mean bias from −10.2% to +0.8%, but worsened 21-session calibration. A one-session delay retained a 23.2% 21-session QLIKE advantage over HAR.

How to interpret the evidence

Later correction results are exploratory; mean-bias improvement is not full calibration or a confirmed loss winner. Staleness sensitivity does not prove historical vendor delivery. The target is a variance proxy; VRP is not identified as the cause of bias. New-period confirmation remains outstanding.
