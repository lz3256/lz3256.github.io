Self-Supervised Trade-Event Representations: Pretraining vs. Transfer

Next-event pretraining, history-window design, and the boundaries of cross-stock transfer.

Research update · October 13, 2026

Market microstructureTransformers
Repository ↗October 2026 transfer study ↗Paired comparisons ↗Earlier encoding studies ↗

Research question

Do next-event modeling gains transfer to short-horizon relative-volatility classification on unseen stocks?

Work completed

Pretrained a ~2M-parameter joint-event Transformer on 7.9M TAQ training events; froze models and thresholds for event/clock-window comparisons across six stocks and ten additional dates, with date-block bootstrap and three-comparison Holm adjustment.

Data and design

32,162 evaluation anchors on ten additional historical trading dates. Seen stocks: AAPL, AMZN, MSFT; unseen group: NVDA, JPM, XOM. Date-level inference accounts for the dependence of anchors within dates.

Recorded findings

Original next-event NLL improved over Markov by 0.114 / 0.177 nats per event on future-date / unseen-stock splits. On the downstream unseen-stock group, clock windows improved pretrained NLL (paired change −0.028; 95% CI [−0.041, −0.015]). The statistics benchmark was severely miscalibrated on JPM/XOM: 91–94% confidence versus 23–27% accuracy, below 33.3% uniform-random chance.

How to interpret the evidence

Cross-stock transfer remained unestablished: the pretrained model still trailed random encoders and uniform forecasts in the unseen-stock group. Results cover ten dates and condition on fixed models. Pointwise intervals are not Holm-adjusted intervals, and trade likelihood is not trading-profit evidence.
