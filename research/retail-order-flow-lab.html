Retail Order Flow: Measurement Error in Trade Signing

Signing-rule sensitivity and disagreement-based adjustments in retail order-flow signals.

Research update · October 13, 2026

Measurement errorPredictive validation
Repository ↗Research report ↗Aggregate comparisons ↗Data contract ↗

Research question

How much does signing-rule choice change a retail-flow signal, and do disagreement-based adjustments improve return prediction?

Work completed

Compared six matched-sample signal variants in a point-in-time monthly 1,000-stock universe; used 24 Holm-adjusted predictive comparisons, 20 random-deletion controls, and independent-sign-flip sensitivity simulations.

Data and design

258 eligible non-overlapping weekly formation windows over 2020–2025. Inputs use WRDS CRSP and TAQ. Historical period labels are inherited: all periods had already been inspected before the extension.

Recorded findings

BJZZ and quote-midpoint signals had mean cross-sectional rank correlations of 0.50–0.53 across periods; disagreement reweighting barely changed quote-midpoint rankings (ρ = 0.9985–0.9992). The tested adjustments did not reliably improve rank IC or ridge forecast MSE; targeted exclusions did not consistently outperform random deletion.

How to interpret the evidence

Disagreement is not a true signing-error rate. Matched stock samples do not make the two signed-trade sets identical. Baseline rank IC was not significant in the current matched periods. The study does not establish causal measurement-error effects, trading profitability, or a fresh holdout.
